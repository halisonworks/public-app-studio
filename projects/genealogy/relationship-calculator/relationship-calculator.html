Two legal traditions count kinship differently. Civil law adds both lines together; canon law takes only the longer line. Both are measured from the most recent common ancestor.

Descent from the common ancestor

About / How it works. Every blood relationship can be described by a single shared ancestor — the Most Recent Common Ancestor (MRCA). To use this tool, first identify that ancestor, then count how many generations each person sits below them.

From those two counts the relationship follows a simple rule. If one count is 0, the people are in a direct line (parent/child, grandparent/grandchild, …). If both are 1, they are siblings. Otherwise the smaller count sets the cousin degree (first, second, …) and the difference between the counts is how many times they are removed. When the smaller count is 1 you get aunt/uncle and niece/nephew ties.

The "degree of kinship" is shown two ways: the civil (Roman) degree counts every step up to the ancestor and back down (g1 + g2), while the canon (Church) degree counts only the longer line (max of g1, g2).
