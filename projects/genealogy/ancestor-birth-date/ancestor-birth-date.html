Enumeration-date conventions vary: some censuses record age as of a fixed reference night (e.g. UK census night), others as of the day the household was visited. A stated whole-year age implies the birthday had already occurred on or before that date, giving a roughly one-year window.

About this calculator. Birth dates are found by calendar subtraction: days are subtracted first (borrowing whole calendar months at their real length), then months, then years. This mirrors how a "67 years 4 months 11 days" age was originally counted, rather than relying on automatic date overflow.

Month lengths & leap years. Borrowing uses the actual length of each month, with the Gregorian leap rule (divisible by 4, except centuries not divisible by 400). If a borrowed result lands on a day that does not exist in the target month, it is clamped to the last day and the result is flagged as approximate.

Old Style dates (pre-1752). Britain and its colonies used the Julian calendar with a 25 March new year until 1752. Day-of-week and exact spans for early dates may differ from the historical record; treat pre-1752 results with caution and note the calendar in use on the source.

Approximate ages. Gravestone, obituary, and census ages are often rounded. Always confirm a computed birth date against a primary birth or baptism record.
