About — how ahnentafel numbering works

An ahnentafel (German for "ancestor table"), also called the Sosa-Stradonitz system, assigns a single number to every ancestor of one starting person.

  • #1 is the subject — the person whose ancestry is being charted (the de cujus).
  • A person numbered n has father 2n and mother 2n+1.
  • Every man is even and every woman is odd — the only exception is #1, who may be either.
  • The generation is floor(log₂(n)) + 1: #1 is gen 1, #2–3 are gen 2, #4–7 are gen 3, and so on.
  • Write n in binary and drop the leading 1; the remaining bits spell the path from the subject, where 0 = father and 1 = mother, read left to right.

Example: 11 in binary is 1011. Drop the leading 1 to get 011 → father, mother, mother → "your father's mother's mother", i.e. a maternal great-grandmother on the paternal side.
