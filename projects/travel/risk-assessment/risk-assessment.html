What is Risk Assessment?

In Health, Safety and Environment (HMS) work, a risk assessment helps you identify hazards, understand how dangerous they are, and decide what to do about them. The classic formula is:

Risk = Likelihood × Consequence

A rare event with a catastrophic outcome can still carry high risk — the matrix makes this visible. After you add control measures, you re-score the hazard to get the residual risk.

The 1–5 Scales: Likelihood and Consequence

Likelihood (probability)

1Very unlikely — Could happen but rarely does (e.g. once every few years)
2Unlikely — Known to happen occasionally
3Possible — Could happen; similar events are known
4Likely — Will probably happen; has happened before
5Almost certain — Expected to happen repeatedly

Consequence (severity)

1Negligible — No injury; minor inconvenience
2Minor — First-aid treatment; slight disruption
3Moderate — Medical treatment; temporary harm
4Serious — Hospitalisation; lasting injury
5Catastrophic — Death or permanent severe disability
The 5×5 Matrix & Risk Zones

Multiply Likelihood × Consequence to get the risk score (1–25). The score falls into one of three zones:

Low
Score 1–4
Acceptable. Monitor and maintain controls.
Medium
Score 5–12
Act to reduce risk. Assign responsibility & deadline.
High
Score 15–25
Immediate action required or stop the activity.

Note: score 13 and 14 don't exist in this scale (4×4=16, so products jump from 12 to 15). The banding is: 1–4 = Low, 5–12 = Medium, 15–25 = High.

Hierarchy of Measures

When you choose controls to reduce risk, always try the most effective options first. Work down this hierarchy:

1
Eliminate — Remove the hazard entirely. Can you stop doing the task, or change the process so the hazard no longer exists?
2
Substitute — Replace the hazard with something less dangerous (e.g. swap a chemical cleaner for a safer product).
3
Technical / Engineering — Add physical barriers, guards, improved drainage, ventilation or other engineered controls.
4
Administrative / Procedures — Safe work instructions, training, supervision, signage, shift limits, lone-worker check-ins.
5
PPE — Personal Protective Equipment (gloves, hi-vis, hard hat). Always the last resort — it doesn't remove the hazard.

Measures reduce likelihood (e.g. training, barriers) and/or consequence (e.g. PPE, emergency procedures). After measures, re-score to find the residual risk.

Worked Examples from Travel & Service

🏨 Slippery hotel lobby floor

Initial riskLikelihood 4 × Consequence 3 = 12 Medium
MeasuresAnti-slip matting (technical), wet floor signs (admin), staff spill-response procedure (admin)
Residual riskLikelihood 2 × Consequence 3 = 6 Medium → 2×2=4 Low after further improvement

🌙 Lone worker at reception overnight

Initial riskLikelihood 2 × Consequence 4 = 8 Medium
MeasuresRegular check-in procedure (admin), panic button / duress alarm (technical), CCTV (technical)
Residual riskLikelihood 2 × Consequence 2 = 4 Low

🌿 Guest allergic reaction on a tour

Initial riskLikelihood 2 × Consequence 5 = 10 Medium (close to High)
MeasuresPre-tour allergy questionnaire (admin), guide carries EpiPen (PPE/admin), first-aid trained guide (admin)
Residual riskLikelihood 1 × Consequence 4 = 4 Low
