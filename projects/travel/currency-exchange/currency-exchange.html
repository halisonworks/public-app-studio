Currency Exchange & the Spread

Why do you always get less money back than you changed?

Why do you always lose money at a currency exchange?

Every time you exchange money — at an airport kiosk, a bank, or an ATM abroad — you lose a little. Even if you change back the same amount you started with, you end up with less than you began with. That's not a glitch. It's a built-in margin called the spread.

The mid-market rate (also called the interbank rate) is the midpoint between the price traders are willing to buy and sell a currency for on wholesale markets. It's what you see on Google or XE.com — and it's not the rate you get at a bureau.

Example: The mid-market rate for EUR is 11.50 kr per €1. This is the "fair" reference price. Bureaux add a margin around it.

A bureau quotes two rates. Remember: the terms are from the bureau's perspective.

Sell rate
The bureau sells you foreign currency.
You pay more kr per unit than mid. This is the worse rate when you're going abroad.
Buy rate
The bureau buys foreign currency back from you.
You receive fewer kr per unit than mid. This is the worse rate when you return home.

With a 2.5% spread: EUR mid = 11.50 → sell rate ≈ 11.79 kr, buy rate ≈ 11.21 kr.

The spread is the gap between the sell rate and buy rate, expressed as a % of mid. A ±2.5% spread means the bureau earns about 5% on a round trip.

sell rate = mid × (1 + spread%)
buy rate = mid × (1 − spread%)
e.g. 11.50 × 1.025 = 11.79 | 11.50 × 0.975 = 11.21

Airports and tourist centres often charge 3–5%. Banks are typically 1–3%. Some fintech cards use the true mid-market rate with a flat fee.

On top of the spread, many bureaux charge a fixed fee (e.g. 35 kr per transaction) and/or a commission % (e.g. 0.5% of the amount). These hit small transactions hardest.

Changing 500 kr with a 35 kr fee = 7% gone just in fees before the spread even applies. Changing 5 000 kr = only 0.7% in fees.

Assumptions: EUR mid = 11.50 kr, spread ±2.5%, fixed fee 30 kr.

Key Terms

Mid-market rate
The midpoint of wholesale buy/sell prices. The "fair" reference rate.
Spread
The % margin above and below mid that the bureau applies to buy and sell rates.
Sell rate
Rate at which the bureau sells foreign currency to you (higher than mid = worse for you).
Buy rate
Rate at which the bureau buys foreign currency from you (lower than mid = worse for you).
Commission
A % fee charged on the full transaction amount, on top of the spread.
Fixed fee
A flat kr fee per transaction, regardless of amount (hurts small transactions most).
